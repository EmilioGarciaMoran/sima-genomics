**Genomic Identity of the Sima de los Huesos Hominins: Convergent Evidence from Mitochondrial and Nuclear DNA**

# **Abstract**

**Objectives. **The Sima de los Huesos (SH) hominin assemblage (~430 ka, Atapuerca, Spain) occupies a critical phylogenetic node in Middle Pleistocene paleoanthropology, yet the apparent discordance between its mitochondrial and nuclear genomic signals has remained unresolved. We present an integrated reanalysis of both genomic compartments using distance-economy, maximum likelihood, and topological hypothesis testing to determine the phylogenetic placement of the SH population and assess its taxonomic status.

**Materials and Methods. **Six complete mitochondrial genomes (16,568 bp) were aligned with MAFFT v7.481 and subjected to distance-economy comparison, maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic reconstruction, and topology testing. ML analysis was performed in IQ-TREE v3.0.1 (HKY+G4 model; log-likelihood −30678.61) and RAxML-NG v1.2.2 (GTR+G model; log-likelihood −30667.34), each with 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates. Kishino-Hasegawa (KH) topology testing was conducted in IQ-TREE (10,000 replicates) evaluating two competing hypotheses: (H0) Sima basal sister to (Denisova + Neandertal), and (H1) (Sima + Neandertal) grouped versus Denisova. Nuclear D-statistics (ABBA-BABA) were computed with ANGSD v0.941 on chromosome 1 (218,757,031 filtered sites) using three topological configurations, with block jackknife error correction and post-mortem damage filtering. Pairwise nuclear distances were estimated from ~2.19 million subsampled sites; a Neighbor-Joining tree was rooted on Ust'-Ishim.

**Results. **The SH mitochondrial lineage is a basal sister to the (Denisova + Neandertal) clade with strong support (IQ-TREE bootstrap 93%, RAxML bootstrap 100%). Maximum likelihood estimates converge on the same topology (IQ-TREE: log-likelihood −30678.608; RAxML-NG: log-likelihood −30667.336), with high bootstrap support at internal nodes (Neandertal + Feldhofer: 100%; ((Neandertal, Feldhofer) + Mezmaiskaya): 100%). The Kishino-Hasegawa test supports the mtDNA-derived topology (H0) over the nDNA-derived alternative (H1). Nuclear D-statistics demonstrate unambiguous greater allele sharing between SH and Neandertals than between SH and Denisovans (D = 0.7425 vs. 0.6705; Z = 30.6 vs. 23.6; p < 2 × 10⁻¹⁶). The Sima–Neandertal pairwise nuclear distance (0.001376) is 1.82× shorter than Sima–Denisova (0.002506).

**Discussion. **The mito-nuclear discordance is not paradoxical: the maternal lineage predates the Neandertal–Denisova split, while the nuclear genome places SH firmly within the Neandertal stem lineage. These data are incompatible with assignment of the SH population to a generic *Homo heidelbergensis*, and support the recognition of ***Homo atapuercae*** (Bermúdez de Castro et al., 1997) as a distinct European taxon representing a basal population ancestral to later Neandertals. ML and topology-testing results provide independent confirmation of the mtDNA phylogeny.

**Keywords: ***Homo atapuercae*; ancient DNA; D-statistics; maximum likelihood phylogeny; Middle Pleistocene; mito-nuclear discordance

# **1. Introduction**

The site of Sima de los Huesos at Atapuerca (Burgos, Spain) has yielded the largest assemblage of Middle Pleistocene hominin fossils known anywhere in the world — at least 28 individuals, predominantly attributed to the morphological taxon *Homo heidelbergensis* or, alternatively, early Neandertals. The antiquity of the deposit (~430 ka by U-series and thermoluminescence dating) places these hominins approximately 380,000 years before the earliest well-documented Neandertals and ~360,000 years before the youngest Denisovans.

The recovery of ancient mitochondrial DNA from SH specimen Femur XIII (Meyer et al., 2014) produced an unexpected result: the mtDNA phylogenetically grouped with Denisovans rather than Neandertals, despite the morphological and geographic affinities of the SH hominins with the Neandertal lineage. This finding was interpreted as evidence of either ancient hybridization, incomplete lineage sorting, or a more complex Middle Pleistocene population structure than previously envisioned.

Subsequent recovery of nuclear genomic data from the same specimen (Meyer et al., 2016) suggested nuclear affinity with Neandertals, but the limited coverage (~0.02×) and analytical scope of that study left the question open to further investigation. Here we present a comprehensive reanalysis using current bioinformatic tools (ANGSD v0.941, IQ-TREE v3.0.1, RAxML-NG v1.2.2) and maximum likelihood phylogenetic methods, integrating findings from both genomic compartments into a unified phylogenetic interpretation.

## **2.2 Mitochondrial phylogenetic reconstruction**

Mitochondrial genomes were aligned using MAFFT v7.481 (Katoh & Standley, 2013) with default parameters. Three complementary phylogenetic approaches were employed:

**Distance-economy analysis.** Pairwise Jukes-Cantor distances were calculated, and trees were constructed under two competing topological hypotheses (Sima basal vs. Sima+Neandertal) with 1,000 bootstrap replicates using the PHYLIP package (Felsenstein, 2005). Tree length differences (Δ) were calculated to assess which topology required fewer evolutionary steps.

**Maximum likelihood (IQ-TREE).** ML phylogenetic reconstruction was performed using IQ-TREE v3.0.1 (Minh et al., 2020). The HKY+G4 model was selected based on standard phylogenetic model testing. Analysis parameters: 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates; seed 12345; automatic thread selection (optimized to 2 threads on 8-core hardware). Final log-likelihood: −30678.608. Bootstrap support values were calculated across 1,000 replicate trees.

**Maximum likelihood (RAxML-NG).** Independent ML analysis was performed using RAxML-NG v1.2.2 (Kozlov et al., 2019). The GTR+G model was applied with Felsenstein Bootstrap (1,000 replicates). Final log-likelihood: −30667.336. Bootstrap support values were computed by evaluating the 1,000 bootstrap trees against the original alignment.

**Topology testing.** Competing topologies were evaluated using the Kishino-Hasegawa (KH) test as implemented in IQ-TREE. Two constraint trees were defined:
- H0 (mtDNA hypothesis): (Sima, (Denisova, ((Neandertal, Feldhofer), Mezmaiskaya)))
- H1 (nDNA hypothesis): ((Sima, Neandertal), (Denisova, ((Feldhofer, Mezmaiskaya), Pan)))

The KH test was conducted with 10,000 bootstrap replicates and the HKY+G4 model. Approximately Unbiased (AU) test p-values were computed for each hypothesis.

The distance-economy approach recovered two trees with markedly different topologies. Under the hypothesis that Sima branches basal to (Denisova + Neandertal), the tree required 82.8 steps. Under the alternative hypothesis (Sima + Neandertal), the tree required 165.6 steps—a difference of 82.8 steps, favoring the first topology with overwhelming parsimony-based support (bootstrap 1,000/1,000).

# **4. Discussion**

The mito-nuclear discordance observed in Sima de los Huesos is not paradoxical but reflects distinct genealogical histories of the mitochondrial and nuclear genomes. Our maximum likelihood and topology testing results provide independent confirmation that the mtDNA phylogeny (Sima basal to Denisova + Neandertal) is robust to different inference methods and model assumptions. The convergence of three independent phylogenetic approaches (distance-economy, IQ-TREE ML, RAxML-NG ML) on the same topology, combined with high bootstrap support (93–100%) and consistent log-likelihood values, demonstrates that this phylogenetic placement is well-supported by the mitochondrial sequence data.

In contrast, the nuclear genome places SH firmly within the Neandertal stem lineage, as evidenced by D-statistics (Z = 30.6, p < 2 × 10⁻¹⁶) and pairwise distance measures. This discordance can be explained by the deeper coalescence time of mtDNA lineages compared to nuclear genome lineages, combined with the particular demographic history of the SH population and its descendants.

These data are incompatible with assignment of the SH population to a generic *Homo heidelbergensis* and support the recognition of ***Homo atapuercae*** (Bermúdez de Castro et al., 1997) as a distinct European taxon representing a basal population ancestral to later Neandertals or occupying a position within the early Neandertal lineage.

# **5. Limitations**

**Genomic coverage.** mtDNA analyses are based on complete mitochondrial genomes (16,568 bp), providing reliable phylogenetic signal. Nuclear analyses are based on chromosome 1 only (~8% of the autosomal genome), reflecting the ~0.02× coverage of the Sima specimen. Chromosome 1 yields 218,757,031 filtered sites with Z-scores far exceeding conventional significance thresholds; internal consistency across three independent metrics supports representativeness. Extension to additional chromosomes is warranted.

**Single nuclear specimen.** Nuclear DNA was recovered from a single individual (Femur XIII). Population-level representativeness cannot be assessed.

**Mitochondrial single-locus evidence.** Despite exceptional bootstrap support (IQ-TREE 93%, RAxML 100%), mtDNA represents one gene tree. This is why the nuclear D-statistics constitute the primary phylogenetic inference for population affinity.

**Outgroup choice.** Ust'-Ishim carries ~2% documented Neandertal ancestry. Sensitivity analyses with alternative outgroups are warranted.

**Taxonomic interpretation.** The application of *Homo atapuercae* is presented as a hypothesis consistent with these data, not a formal taxonomic revision.

# **6. Conclusions**

Integrated analysis of mitochondrial (6 complete genomes; maximum likelihood analysis with 1,000 bootstrap replicates in IQ-TREE and RAxML-NG; log-likelihoods: IQ-TREE −30678.61, RAxML −30667.34) and nuclear (chromosome 1; 218,757,031 sites; D-statistics with jackknife error correction) DNA from Sima de los Huesos demonstrates that this population occupies a basal sister position to all known Neandertals and Denisovans in the mitochondrial genealogy (IQ-TREE bootstrap 93%, RAxML bootstrap 98–100%), while its nuclear genome shows unambiguous preferential affinity with Neandertals (D = 0.7425, Z = 30.6, p < 2 × 10⁻¹⁶). These concordant signals—mtDNA phylogeny supported by distance-economy, maximum likelihood, and topology testing; nuclear genome strongly affiliated with Neandertal—support the recognition of the Sima de los Huesos hominins as a distinct European taxon, *Homo atapuercae*, representing a stem population ancestral to or within the Neandertal lineage, with deep roots in western Europe extending back to at least the early Middle Pleistocene.
